**Project:** SNAP Analytics Platform  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Author:** Saki Chen

---

# Purpose

This document explains **how the SNAP Analytics Platform is designed** and, more importantly, **why each component exists**.

The architecture is business-driven rather than technology-driven. Every layer is designed to answer business questions with trusted, reusable analytics.

---

# 1. Design Philosophy

The platform follows one simple principle:

> **Business problems drive architecture.**

Technology is a tool—not the starting point.

The design begins with business operations, then progressively transforms operational data into trusted business metrics that support decision-making.

# 3. Layer Responsibilities

| Layer | Purpose | Why It Exists |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| Business Operations | Real-world program activities | Analytics should begin with business workflows. |
| Operational Data | Source datasets | Captures business events generated by operations. |
| Data Pipeline | Collect, clean, and transform data | Standardizes raw data for downstream use. |
| Data Quality Validation | Verify completeness and correctness | Prevents unreliable data from reaching decision makers. |
| Business Data Model | Organize business entities | Creates a reusable analytical foundation. |
| Business Metrics Layer | Centralize KPI definitions | Ensures every dashboard uses the same business logic. |
| Analytics Dashboard | Present trusted insights | Helps stakeholders understand performance. |
| Decision Support | Enable informed decisions | Analytics exists to improve business decisions. |

---

# 4. Data Flow

The platform processes data through a series of stages.

1. Public program data is collected.
2. Raw data is cleaned and standardized.
3. Validation rules identify potential quality issues.
4. Business entities are modeled into analytical datasets.
5. Standardized KPIs are calculated.
6. Dashboards visualize trusted business metrics.
7. Stakeholders use the insights to support decisions.

---

# 5. Key Design Decisions

## Separate Data Quality from Transformation

Data cleaning and data validation serve different purposes.

- Transformation prepares the data.
- Validation builds trust in the data.

Keeping these responsibilities separate makes issues easier to identify and maintain.

---

## Centralize Business Metrics

Business calculations should not live inside dashboards.

Instead, KPIs are defined once in a reusable metrics layer.

Benefits:

- Consistent KPI definitions
- Easier maintenance
- Reusable across dashboards
- Single source of truth

---

## Business-First Architecture

The architecture is organized around business concepts rather than technical components.

The design starts with questions such as:

- What business decision needs support?
- What operational data is required?
- How should success be measured?

Technology choices follow those answers.

# Architecture Principles

The architecture follows five guiding principles:

1. Business before technology.
2. Data quality before visualization.
3. Reusable business logic.
4. Clear separation of responsibilities.
5. Design for maintainability and scalability.
